# Funnel videos: notes (Nov 7, 2026)

Five transcripts the owner shared about e-commerce funnels. All five come
from agencies or coaches selling a service, course or tool; their revenue
numbers and conversion rates are unverified. Their shared ideas are what we
use.

| # | Source | Core idea |
|---|--------|-----------|
| F1 | "Mark Builds Brands", ad → advertorial → sales page → order page → upsells | Only ~3% of a market is ready to buy now; a product-page-only funnel speaks only to them. Pre-sell the rest (problem state → solution state) before the sale. Raise order value with bundles, bumps and post-purchase upsells ("solve one problem, create the next"). Recover drop-off with email/SMS. |
| F2 | Framer-sponsored creator, funnels for services/info products | Capture leads before asking for the sale: lead magnet → squeeze page → thank-you page → automated emails. Tripwire (cheap first purchase, then a bigger offer). DM funnels. Test three copy angles: personal narrative, evidence, direct response. Keep a swipe file of landing pages. |
| F3 | Email/SMS agency, "the 5 decisions" | The customer decides 5 times: (1) do I trust this brand enough to click (profile as landing page, partnership/whitelisted ads) (2) is this for me (a page matched to the ad's one pain point; honest quizzes) (3) buy now or later (strong offer, proof, popup email/SMS capture, 3–7 day decision cycle, welcome flow by answer) (4) was it a good decision (founder note, how-to, check-ins) (5) should I come back (timely reorder reasons, word of mouth). |
| F4 | Haze Media, full e-com funnel masterclass | Research → personas → angles → one awareness level per ad. Whitelisting; real variety for Andromeda. **Landing page by awareness:** unaware/problem-aware → advertorial; solution-aware → listicle; product-aware → listicle or product page; most aware → product page. AOV × conversion rate ≥ 250 as a health check. Real urgency, scarcity, risk reversal, gifts; cart cross-sells; shipping insurance; post-purchase flows timed to real reorder behaviour; VIP segment. |
| F5 | Agency breakdown of Grüns | Congruence: one avatar → its own ads, landing page **and offer**. Specific avatars in markets big enough to scale. **Test an avatar with ads to the general page first; build the custom funnel only once it shows signal** (~6 angles per avatar). Iterations sustain; macro moves scale: new avatars, reaching unaware people, better revenue per visitor, then new offers/products/markets. ~60–70% exploit, 30–40% explore. Stack observations. |

## Where they agree

1. Most of the market isn't ready to buy; the money is in moving unaware and
   problem-aware people (F1, F4, F5), and that needs more steps, not fewer.
2. The page must continue the ad's exact reason for clicking (F3, F4, F5).
3. Most visitors won't buy on the first visit: capture email/SMS and bring
   them back (F1, F2, F3, F4).
4. Order value and post-purchase matter as much as the first sale (F1, F3, F4).
5. Persona-specific beats broad (F3, F4, F5), with real creative variety (F4).

## Where they differ, and what we take

| Topic | Positions | We take |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| When to build custom pages | F1/F4: always pre-sell cold traffic. F5: test with the general page first, build for winners. | **F5's order.** Test week → general page + cheap capture; custom pages only for winning personas. |
| Friction | F1: more steps make better buyers. F4: don't interrupt checkout. | Both: more steps *before* the decision (education), none *inside* checkout. |
| Subscriptions | F3/F4/F5: the main LTV lever. | Doesn't fit reusable patches. Our repeat purchase = new areas, gifts, replacement sets. |
| Urgency | F4: timers and scarcity. | Real deadlines only (e.g. the Black Friday end date). Fake timers get called out in comments, which we measure. |
