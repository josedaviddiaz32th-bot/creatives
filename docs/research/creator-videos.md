# Creator videos: notes and contradictions (Nov 7, 2026)

Seven short videos the owner collected on creative strategy. Transcribed
locally with Whisper and checked against 16 frames each. These are creators
selling courses or communities; every video ends in a lead magnet ("comment
X", "DM me"). Their numbers ($6M/month, $300k in two weeks) are unverified
credentials, not evidence. Treat their advice as claims to weigh against the
web research (`creative-strategy-web-research.md`) and the owner's handoff.

## The videos

| # | Creator | Length | Format | Core claim |
|---|---------|--------|--------|------------|
| 1 | @dakota.hermes (AdClass) | 2:57 | Motion-graphics explainer, voiceover | **The Angle Matrix.** An angle = one reason to buy, for one person, at one awareness stage. A new hook or edit on the same pitch is the same angle. 8 desires (Whitman's "Life Force 8") × 5 Schwartz stages = 40 angles per offer. Each angle opens a new pocket of the audience; "the angle is the targeting". Test each angle in its own ad set with 3–5 versions; spend 2–3× target cost before judging. Expect most angles to fail. |
| 2 | @dakota.hermes | 3:02 | Same | **The hook filters.** Its second job is to push away the wrong people. Meta learns from whoever converts, so the copy does the targeting. A narrow callout ("if you've ever been stood up at an open house") beats a broad one ("Attention business owners"). Best CTR can be the worst ad. Then: trust line, then a real offer as CTA. |
| 3 | unnamed media buyer | 1:43 | Talking head + article | **Retrieval before ranking.** Andromeda first retrieves candidate ads, then ranks them. An ad that fails retrieval never competes, so bids, page and offer don't matter for it. (Matches Meta's engineering post; "your bids don't matter" is overstated.) |
| 4 | @ryanecom_ | 1:15 | Talking head, hoodie, earbuds | **Native ads + volume.** Rip competitors' proven copy and iterate it. The first 2–3 lines must name the avatar and the pain. Iterate winners AND launch 5–10 new concepts every night. An ad set Meta won't spend on is a loser; study why. |
| 5 | @ericdoesecom | 1:51 | Talking head, cap | **Long-form AI VSL.** ~2 min, authority-figure persona. Number-driven hook → 30s problem and symptoms → 30s "not your fault, why other fixes failed" → 30s mechanism → product only in the last 30s ("looks less like a scammy ad", more watch time). | 6 | @datosmotion (Spanish) | 1:05 | 3D roller-coaster motion graphics, burned-in captions | **8 pieces, always in order:** hook → intro (what they get if they stay) → body → **open loop** ("but that's not the craziest part") → body → loop → body → CTA. | 7 | @creativewithchris | 0:52 | Talking head + green-screen examples | **10/10 creative funnel** (example brand: Primal Queen). ## Where they agree (and the research agrees)

- **The creative does the targeting.** Videos 1, 2, 3 and 4, plus Meta's own
  statements: what the ad says and shows decides who it reaches.
- **Name the person and her pain early.** Videos 2, 4 and 5; the owner's
  avatar doc already writes hooks this way ("In your 30s and…?").
- **Real variety beats cosmetic variety.** Video 1 ("a new hook is the same
  angle") matches Meta's "similar ads count as one creative".
- **Funnel stages need different jobs and formats.** Videos 1, 5 and 7
  match Schwartz and the practitioner mapping in the web research.
- **Late product reveal at the top of the funnel.** Videos 5 and 7.

## Contradictions, and what we pick

| Topic | Says A | Says B | We go with | Why |
|-------|--------|--------|------------|-----|
| What the hook must be | Owner: a question. | Video 2: a filtering callout. Video 6: the hook isn't even the most important piece. | **A question that is also a callout** ("In your 30s and dodging group photos?"). Plus open loops in any video over ~25s. | Owner's rule stays, and a question can filter. The avatar doc already does both at once. Open loops are cheap and fit our story format. |
| When the product appears | Lessons §3: product on screen in the first 1–2s. | Videos 5 and 7: product only after 30s+ at TOF. | **Depends on stage.** TOF: product not spoken until the second half; it may sit in frame (nightstand) without comment. MOF: product early, it's the subject. BOF: product and offer in second one. | Both are right for different awareness levels. Schwartz: unaware people need a story first, product-aware people need the offer. |
| Is a new hook a new ad? | Lessons §3: "a remix with a new first frame and new hook is a legitimate new test". Video 4: iterate winners. | Video 1 + Meta: same angle with a new hook is the same concept; it finds the same people. | **Both, labelled differently.** A hook swap is a *hook test* (measures hook rate). It never counts as a new *concept* in the matrix. Scaling a winner = same angle, **new format**, face or setting. | Organic posts don't go through Andromeda, so hook swaps still teach us something this week. But the strategy must be ready for paid, where they collapse into one. |
| Who's on camera | Video 5: an authority figure, run from an authority page. | Video 7: creators who look like the customer. Lessons: real-looking beats polished; no dermatologist claims. | **Peer, not fake expert.** She looks like the persona. | An AI "doctor" or invented expert breaks our claim rules and is what gets flagged. A peer can still sound informed. |
| Video length | Owner's first spec: 30–35s. | Video 5: ~2 min VSLs. Video 7: TOF 30s+ before the product. | **Length by stage:** BOF 15–25s, MOF 25–40s, TOF 35–60s. One long-form test in week 1 (slot 4-T, 45–60s). | Owner already lifted the fixed length. Long-form costs more credits per video (see strategy). |
| How many concepts | Video 4: 5–10 new concepts every night. | Video 1: 3–5 versions per angle per ad set. Research: 4–6 concepts per ad set. | **3 videos a day, each a different concept**, ~15–18 in the test week. | We're bounded by Higgsfield credits and review time, not ad spend. Fewer, clearly different concepts give cleaner reads. |
| How to judge | Video 4: Meta's spend allocation is the verdict. Video 1: spend 2–3× target cost. | Video 2: CTR can mislead; judge by who converts. | **Organic metrics by stage** (see strategy). Bio-link clicks and Shopify sessions beat views. | No paid spend this week, so spend-based rules don't apply. Video 2's warning maps to: don't crown a winner on views. |
| Format of BOF | Video 7: static images. | Our pipeline: video only. | **Video for this test**, styled like a static (offer card, product, one line, 10–15s). | Static images can't be compared on watch time; a near-static short video can. |

## Not useful for us

- Video 5's tool (makewinners.ai) and its "authority Facebook page": we
  generate on Higgsfield, and a fake authority page conflicts with our claims rules.
- Video 4's "rip competitors' copy": we copy formats only, never words
  (trend-scout rule and lessons §3).
- Ad-set structure advice (videos 1 and 4) only matters once there's paid spend.
