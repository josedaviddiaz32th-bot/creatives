# Lessons learned: Wrinkle Smooth Patches

Source: the owner's "Creative Generation Handoff – Wrinkle Smooth Patches"
(Nov 7, 2026), full PDF in `docs/creative-handoff-wrinkle-smooth.pdf`. Every
agent that writes or judges a story or prompt reads this file, and it overrides
agent defaults.

**Precedence:** the owner's direct instructions (section 0) > this doc > live
Shopify / product page > older research. The persona doc ("Avatars" in Google
Drive) is the best source of the customer's own words and angles, but **only
section 2 here sets what we may claim about the product.**

## 0. Owner's direct instructions for video (override the handoff where they differ)

- **Story first.** Concept → story → story review → THEN storyboard and prompts.
- **Hook = first ~5s and it asks a question** that resonates with this specific persona.
- **Body ~20s:** go deeper into the problem, then turn *smoothly* to the
  patches. No "struggling with wrinkles and tried everything? Well, I have these
  patches for you." The patches arrive as part of the story.
- **CTA: subtle.** "Check the bio" / "try it". "Buy" is OK only if it's said subtly.
- **Length depends on the funnel stage** (Nov 7, 2026): TOF 30–50s, MOF
  25–45s, BOF 15–25s (`pipeline/config.yaml`). Generated on Higgsfield.
- **Every video fills one slot of `docs/creative-strategy.md`** (persona ×
  awareness × funnel stage × format). High format variety; a new hook on the
  same video is a hook test, not a new concept.
- **One video starts an idea and ends it.** No "part 2" or sequels.
- **TOF: get me interested. MOF: help me choose. BOF: give me a reason to
  buy.** TOF is for unaware or problem-aware people and is mostly about the
  problem, but it subtly shows the product as the answer and that we're a
  brand, never just a tips page. MOF is solution-aware.
- **Fast-paced edits** (Nov 7, 2026): cuts, punch-ins, pop-ups, word-by-word
  captions and SFX; pauses trimmed; a mild whole-clip speed-up (≤1.08×,
  video and audio together, pitch kept) is allowed. Spec: `editing` in config.
- **One generation per shot** (Nov 7, 2026): every job makes exactly one
  output, no variations or batches. A shot that fails a gate is not redone
  automatically; the run stops and the owner decides. Exception: still
  fixes (one edit per failed still) may run while the run's still-fix spend
  stays within 3 credits.
- **No talking with patches on.** Applying them should look organic (she
  presses one on while holding the phone), never a posed, silent lying-down shot.
- **Purpose:** a 5–6 day organic test on TikTok, Instagram and Facebook,
  built as if they were Meta ads. Winners get iterated in a new format.
- **Talking on camera = voice generated by the same video model in the same
  clip.** ElevenLabs only for voiceover-only videos.
- Owner approves the first rounds before anything posts.
- **Before/afters are allowed** (Nov 7, 2026), including AI-generated ones,
  in any format.
- **Animated shots say "Pixar style"** (Nov 8, 2026): any animated or
  cartoon element (explainer cutaways, a skin cross-section, a mascot, an
  animated character) is prompted with the words "Pixar style" (3D-animated,
  soft rounded shapes, warm cinematic light). Realistic UGC shots stay
  photorealistic.
- **Think like a marketer first** (Nov 8, 2026): before mapping a reference,
  explain in plain words how it makes a COLD viewer understand the problem
  in its first 3–5 s. Example: the car video shows her head banging the window
  while she tries to sleep, so the problem is obvious without context. Our
  version must make an unaware viewer connect cause and effect just as fast,
  e.g. a night-after-night time-lapse of her face pressed into the pillow,
  then bathroom lights on and she notices the lines. A close-up of marks
  with no cause shown could read as scratches. Pass test: describe the
  problem from the first 3 s alone, as a stranger would.
- **Real reactions, never exaggerated** (Nov 8, 2026): frustration or
  sadness is subtle and believable (a sigh, a frown, a long look). No
  screaming, wailing or overacted crying: "It just doesn't click."
- **Match the reference's medium** (Nov 8, 2026): if the reference is all live
  footage, every shot is a video clip. Never stand in stills with zoom or shake
  for live-action beats ("it's supposed to be all video"). Stills are only
  start/end frames for clips, or for formats that are stills in the reference.
- **Real people match the animated character** (Nov 8, 2026): in a video
  that mixes Pixar and photoreal shots, the photoreal person is the same
  person as the character (same ethnicity, hair and age).
- **Default voiceover voice: Higgsfield Zoe** (Nov 8, 2026; seed_audio, preset
  `d0374db1-44b9-4f05-939e-0a9ae9dbbe6a`, speech_rate 15), unless the owner
  names another.
- **Captions: one caption, at most 2 hashtags, tailored to the avatar**
  (Nov 8, 2026).
- **Reviewer severity** (Nov 8, 2026): anatomy quirks a viewer doesn't notice
  at phone speed (see-through teeth, a mouth opening with no speech, a brief
  artifact the edit can hide) are notes, not FAILs. See
  `learning/owner-feedback.md`.
- **Length can be set by the owner per video** (Nov 8, 2026: "about 45
  seconds" for the Resilia-reference run); it overrides the stage band.

How to merge with the handoff's 5 beats (section 4): the beats still apply
*inside* the owner's structure. Hook (question) → problem → patch as the answer
(offer + how it works) → proof/risk reversal → subtle CTA. The handoff's visual
rules still hold: motion in frame one, short on-screen text, and **the patch can
appear visually early** (e.g. she's wearing it, or it's on the nightstand) even
though the script doesn't *talk* about it until the turn. That gets the product
on screen early without a hard verbal pivot.

## 2. Who buys it, and what we may claim

She rejects an **expression**, not her age: she wants to look **rested, not
angry, and still look like herself.** Core buyer 35–55.

**Situations, in priority order:**
1. **"My frown lines make me look angry."** Fine with aging, done with the expression. Wants a relaxed brow without a frozen look.
2. **The side sleeper:** creases on forehead/eyes/chest no longer fade by midday. "It's my pillow, not me." One angle, not every post.
3. **The needle-avoider:** priced out by injectables ($435–$586 a visit, every 3–4 months), scared of needles or a done look, or medically unable.
4. **Burned before:** tried tape or a patch that fell off by 3am or stopped sticking after 2–3 uses. Fall-off is the category's #1 complaint (36% of sampled reviews).

**Her own words (use as language and hooks):** "make me look angry all the
time" · "I need them gone" · "I'm trying everything I can before botox because
the price, and I'm terrified" · "woke up with it screwed up on my bedside table"
· "they do seem to blur wrinkles after waking" · "noticed a difference after the
first night" · "This ad… can't be real" (her verdict on over-edited before/afters).

**The beliefs to move her through, ONE per post, in this order:**
1. Holding the skin flat and sealed in moisture all night **visibly smooths lines by morning**, with nothing going under the skin. (North star.)
2. The patches she tried failed because they fell off or weren't shaped for each area, not because patches don't work. This kit covers the whole face.
3. It's gentle enough for every night, even under the eyes, and takes 30 seconds.
4. One reusable kit is smarter money than tape refills, knockoffs or $435+ injections.
5. The Daily Glow Co. stands behind it: 30 days to love it or get your money back.

Optional opener for side-sleeper and chest: a lot of her lines are pressed in
every night while she sleeps, so they're something she can stop.

**What we can say (owner loosened this Nov 7, 2026):** any wording that is
true of how the patches work and what they do, in the strongest natural
words: "smoother skin by morning", "softens your lines", "stops the creases
you press in every night", "wake up looking rested". The on-screen persona
may talk about her own results in first person ("my lines look so much
softer"); every AI-generated post carries the platform's AI-content label.
An informed peer may explain the mechanism.

**"Removes wrinkles" is approved** (owner, Nov 7, 2026): the patches do
remove wrinkles, just not permanently. Say it freely ("wakes up with the
wrinkles gone", "removes my forehead lines overnight"), but never
"permanent", "forever" or "for good".

**"Lifts sagging skin" is approved too** (owner, Nov 7, 2026).

**Other result claims follow the owner's evidence.** Anything stronger
(lasting results, specific timeframes) is allowed once the owner confirms it
here.

**Authority figures are allowed** (owner, Nov 7, 2026): expert-style
presenters, educators, a confident "skincare nerd", the long-form authority
format. They can look, sound and teach like experts, including a doctor's
look (white coat, clinic setting). Every post carries the platform's
AI-content label.

**Still off limits (fake evidence):** a character stating a credential she
doesn't have, spoken or on screen ("I'm a dermatologist", "Dr. ___", "as a
nurse", an "MD" name tag or caption); made-up statistics, study results
or "X% of women" numbers; fake reviews, star ratings or quotes attributed to
real customers.

**Objections creatives should answer (pick ONE per post):** will it stay on
all night if I toss and turn · will it stop sticking after a few uses · is it
safe under my eyes, will it leave red marks or break me out · is this TikTok
hype, the before/afters look fake · why not just get Botox · why $45 when Amazon
has $13 sets · how do I clean it · how long does shipping take · are the
reviews real, will I really get a refund.

Reddit objections to the category (from the Avatars doc): irritation/rashes
from adhesives, pulling delicate skin when removing, can't use moisturizer
underneath, results are temporary, stiff/smelly/uncomfortable to sleep in,
falls off (especially side sleepers), "sensory nightmare / licking cardboard".

## 3. How to build a post

Five beats (handoff, 15–30s) merged into the owner's structure (section 0; length by funnel stage):

| Beat | Job |
|---|---|
| 1. Hook | Stop the scroll: visual, sound and on-screen text all land in the first 2s. For us it's a **question**. |
| 2. Problem | Name the pain she already feels (looking angry or tired, lines that no longer fade by midday) so she feels seen. |
| 3. Offer | The patch as the answer: what it is, the "aha" of how it works, how easy it is; show the product. |
| 4. Proof & risk reversal | The demo (on at night, off in the morning), the specs, the 30-day money-back guarantee, free shipping. |
| 5. CTA | One action: "Link in bio" or "Comment SMOOTH and I'll send you the link". Subtle. |

**The hook is three things at once:** a visual with motion in frame one (never
a static title card), a sound (voice line, sound effect, product sound) and
on-screen text of **≤8 words** that reads in under a second. If only one can be
great, make it the visual; most people start with sound off. Put her world in
the first second.

**Hook types to draft from (write 5+, keep the strongest 3), rewritten as questions where they fit:**
- Outcome/reveal: "Peel it off. Smoother-looking skin underneath."
- Objection flip: "Finally, a wrinkle patch that actually works." (**owner's favourite**)
- Identification: "Look rested. Still look like you."
- Mechanism: "Holds your skin smooth all night. You just sleep."
- Value/math: "Five pieces. Your whole face." (check per-patch math against live price)
- Pattern interrupt: "Don't buy wrinkle patches until you read this."
- Simplicity: "No needles. No tape. No refills. Just clean and reuse."
- Counterintuitive truth: "Some of the lines you see in the morning were pressed in overnight."
- POV/meme: something she does in private, then the patch.
- Direct callout: "If your frown lines make you look angry, watch this."
- Self-aware: "This is a wrinkle patch video. Give me 10 seconds."

**What makes copy land:** one idea per post (one tension, one promise, one
offer) moving her ONE belief. Specifics beat adjectives: sizes, pieces, price
per patch, guarantee terms. Metaphors make the mechanism instant: "an iron for
your face", "a retainer for your skin". Push one dimension to the edge instead
of being average on all of them. Gaps competitors leave open: the whole face in
one kit, a side-by-side against paper tape, Spanish content, the gift angle
("one for you, one for your mom"). **Real-looking beats polished:** phone
framing, natural light, unretouched skin read as content; glossy reads as an ad
and gets scrolled.

**Adapting a reference post:** break it down first in a table (Element | What
they did | Why it may persuade) covering hook, promise, proof, CTA, mechanism,
offer and 3–5 visual choices. Then map their mechanism → a tension from the
research → the belief shift → our copy and visual. Copy formats and principles,
never a creator's exact words, face or signature bit.

**Brief template (filled in before any generation):**
```
## [Concept name] — [TikTok / Reels], [length] s
Angle: one sentence on the idea and why it should work for her.
Hook variants (same body):
  A. Text: "…" | Visual: … | Sound: …
  B. …
  C. …
Beat sheet: Time | Beat | Visual (asset or prompt) | On-screen text | VO / sound
Caption: problem → solution → CTA, 3–5 hashtags.
Production plan: reused assets (section 7), new generations with model and estimated credits, total cost.
What we're testing: the one variable this post isolates and the metric that decides it.
```

**Video specs:** vertical 1080 × 1920 master; text below the top 14% and above
the bottom 35% (app UI); product on screen in the first 1–2s; **one action per
shot**; voiceover planned at **2.3 words/second** (ElevenLabs narrators read
closer to 2.9), clip lengths set from the word count, never a sped-up voice;
**lips closed or a small closed-lip smile with patches on.**

**Testing:** the hook is the post, so the first 2s get the most effort. Change
one variable at a time (hook, body or CTA). A remix of a working post with a new
first frame and a new hook is a legitimate new test.

## 4. Prompting Higgsfield (stills, characters, product)

**The one rule that fixes most failures: never let a model invent the patch.**
Every prompt that shows the product uploads a real product reference (or uses
the Element) and pastes the PATCHES block word for word.

| Job | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Person + product + pose in one image | Nano Banana 2 / 2.1 (multi-reference) or Elements | Soul 2.0 accepts only ONE reference image, so it can't do person + product |
| Reusing a saved character or product | Elements, written as `<<<element_id>>>` in the prompt | Elements work only on Nano Banana Pro/2, GPT Image 2, Seedream 4.5/5.0 lite, Cinema Studio Image 2.5, and for video Seedance 2.0, Kling 3.0 with a start image, Cinema Studio video |
| Cleaning up a blurry supplier photo | One single-photo cleanup on Nano Banana 2, 4K | "keep outline, don't copy blur, halos or blown-out white, clear frosted, not opaque" |
| Character sheet | Nano Banana 2.1, 4-panel sheet from 3 real photos, then save as an Element | Turns photos of a person into a reusable character |

Cost reference: Nano Banana 2 ≈ 1.5 credits/image; GPT Image 2.6 ≈ 0.25
(checked Nov 7). HEIC isn't accepted; convert to JPG.

**Process that produced approved images:**
1. Start from the owner's avatar or a saved character (section 7), bare-faced, no patches.
2. Product-on-face image = an **edit with two references**, each with one stated role: the avatar as the person, the product render as the product, plus exact placement text.
3. Style changes = an edit of the approved shot: "Keep everything as placed. CHANGES ONLY: …". Never regenerate from scratch to change a colour or outfit.

**Product-only shots:** clear silicone disappears on white, so always shoot on a
tinted surface or against backlight. Brand studio look: mist surface, deep teal
(#17393D) backdrop, sunrise-orange grazing light, a few water droplets. Always
show fingers or a face for scale; a patch alone reads as a chest pad. Never draw
the product as a vector illustration (rejected every time).

**People:** hair pulled back off the forehead so the forehead piece shows; with
patches on, a calm face with lips closed. **The owner banned smiling with patches
on** (it looks unrealistic).

**Statics:** default 4:5 (1080 × 1350). Soft Mint #F4F8F6 background, Poppins,
Deep Teal #17393D text, Sunrise Orange #F08A2C accents. Proof image 60–70% of
frame; headline ≤9 words; put final text on in code.

### Blocks every prompt reuses (paste verbatim)

**PATCHES** (Face Set; swap the shape lines for other products):
```
The patches are the exact clear silicone pieces shown in the product reference, and nothing else. They are thin, transparent and slightly frosted; her skin and lines stay visible through them; they are noticed only by a thin raised edge that catches the light and a soft satin sheen. They are never white, beige, pink, gold, opaque, gel pads, hydrogel, fabric, paper or tape. Forehead piece: a wide, gently curved band covering the middle two-thirds of the forehead, with a small rounded tab at the centre of its lower edge pointing down between the eyebrows; it does not reach the hairline. Under-eye pieces: one slim crescent per eye, just below the lower lashes, following the curve of the lower lid, the outer end reaching toward the outer eye corner; never covering the eye or lashes; never circles, rings or glasses shapes. Smile-line pieces: one slim crescent on each side, along the fold from beside the nostril to beside the mouth corner; the lips stay uncovered. Every patch lies completely flat, with no bubbles, folds or lifted edges. A piece the prompt does not mention is not on her face.
```

**REFERENCE ROLES** (start of every multi-image prompt):
```
Identify the reference photos by what they show, not by upload order. THE CAST PHOTO shows the only person in this image; keep her face, skin, age, hair and earrings exactly as shown. THE PLACEMENT PHOTO shows a different person wearing the patches; use it only for where each piece sits and never copy that person's face, hair or skin. THE SHEET PHOTO shows the five pieces on their backing sheet; use it for exact shape, size and transparency.
```

**STYLE** (end of every realistic image prompt):
```
Photorealistic smartphone photo, unretouched, no beauty filter, visible pores and natural skin texture, sharp focus on her face. Her hair is pulled fully back off her forehead. The skin is dry and matte with no cream or lotion. No mirror in the shot. No text, captions, logos or watermark.
```

## 5. Prompting video (Kling, Seedance, Veo, ElevenLabs)

Video is where the credits go and where most mistakes happened. **Fix the still
before animating it, lock both the start and end frame of every shot that must
connect to another, and give the model one action per clip.**

**Prices seen on Runway** (for reference; Higgsfield prices are in section 9):
Nano Banana Pro 2K 20/image · Kling 3 Pro silent 12/s · Kling 3 Pro + audio
17/s · Gen-4.5 12/s (no end frame; never for chained shots) · Seedance 2.5
20/s draft, 68 at 1080p · Aleph edit 28/s · eleven-music 5–22. Judge models by
**cost per usable shot**.

**Prompting rules:**
1. Each reference gets one role: identity / pose / light, or patch geometry and translucency, or placement only. Say which controls what.
2. Image-to-video prompts describe **motion only**; the image already sets subject, framing and light. Lead with the critical movement.
3. Write motion as timed beats with positions: "0–1 s… 1–3 s… 3–5 s": start position, path, end position, camera locked, what must NOT move, no new objects.
4. Make every end frame unambiguous: the patch fully on, or fully off and held in the fingers. A half-on end frame made the model leave a copy on the skin.
5. **Never pass a multi-panel or split reference image.** The model rebuilds the whole layout (105 credits wasted). Crop to one subject first.
6. Never launch a clip from a start image nobody checked against checklist A (section 10).
7. Two focused attempts per defect, then simplify the motion, change the source or cut around it. (In this pipeline the budget usually allows only one.)

**Continuity method:** design pack first (character sheet, expressions, a night
and a morning colour frame) → storyboard as a chain of keyframes where the end
of shot N is the start of shot N+1, each keyframe an EDIT of the previous one,
with a written state list per frame (patch on or off, what's in frame) →
animate each shot between its start and end frame → voiceover, captions and end
card in assembly.

A motion prompt that worked: "Locked-off macro close-up, camera does not move.
0–1 s… 1–2.5 s… 2.5–4 s… 4–5 s. The eye stays closed and still, lashes do not
move; the patch does not move, bend or change shape; lighting does not change.
Only the crow moves. Smooth, weighty motion; no camera shake, no zoom, no new
objects."

**Talking characters:** voice and picture must be generated together or the
lips won't match (a separate voice laid over a silent clip was rejected). **Don't
write stressed words in ALL CAPS** ("HAPPEN" came out "HA-HAPPEN"); describe
the emphasis instead. If one word glitches, cut it out on frame boundaries
before paying for a re-render. Size a keyed character by its face on screen: at
least 250 px wide on a 1080-wide frame.

**Prompt skeleton that produced usable clips:** a REFERENCE PHOTOS block naming
each photo by what it shows → "the cast photo is a reference for identity only;
it is not the first frame; the video opens directly on shot 1" → THE PERSON:
the character's description word for word, fixed identifiers, hair off the
forehead → "SHE DOES NOT TALK: lips stay closed" (for non-talking shots) → the
PATCHES block → SKIN → CAMERA AND SET (distance, height, light direction,
background objects, no mirror) → timed shots joined by one hard cut → rules
(exactly two hands with five fingers, no fades, no text, audio spec).

**For a talking presenter:** "She says exactly these words, in this order, and
nothing else: …", **the same voice description word for word in every prompt**,
timed gestures, "only her voice and quiet room tone".

**Veo/Gemini notes:** ~8–10s per request; often returns 16:9 even when asked for
vertical (check the first clip).

**ElevenLabs (voiceover-only videos):** model eleven_v3, one MP3 per line,
pauses tightened to ~0.28s, mastered to −16 LUFS. Narrator **Lara** (stability
0.45, style 0.5, similarity 0.75) replaced Niki as more persuasive. Generate
four short transition sounds once (whoosh, soft pop, caption click, short
register ding) and reuse them.

**Code (ffmpeg / Playwright):** use code for overlays (reply bubble, red X,
check-mark pills, price tags, research card), end cards, captions, statics and
all assembly; they cost no credits and render exactly. Don't promise
reference-level 3D from code. Assembly: master at −16 LUFS, music ducked ~10 dB
under speech, J-cuts when a line runs longer than its clip, contact sheet (one
frame every 0.5s) checked before calling it done.

## 6. Peel physics (the hardest shot)

From real footage of a silicone forehead patch, 3.6s. The real motion is a
**fold-back, not a lift**:
1. 0–0.9s: fingertips pinch the outer edge at the temple and lift only that corner a few millimetres.
2. 0.9–2.7s: she pulls the free edge sideways across the forehead, flat against the skin. The peeled part folds back 180° over the part still attached; a straight fold line with a bright highlight travels across the forehead. The doubled part looks slightly frosted; the attached part stays glossy.
3. 2.7–3.2s: the last edge lets go at the other temple. The patch hangs from her fingertips as one soft, curved, translucent sheet.
4. 3.2–3.6s: eyes close briefly, a relaxed exhale.

Skin underneath: dewy, slightly glossy, a few tiny trapped air dots, never red,
never marked. Camera: front-camera selfie, slightly above eye level, 30–35 cm,
gentle handheld sway.

**In prompts write** "folds back over itself", "pulled sideways close to the
skin", "a fold line travels across". **Never write** "lifts away" or "peels off"
on their own: models read them as lifting outward, which breaks the patch. Keep
the peel 3–4s; lock wearing and bare frames; if it fails twice, cut lift → bare
skin.

## 8. Known failures and the fixes

| Failure | Where | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Patch splits, goes rigid like a shell, floats or snaps off during the peel | Gemini peel clips | Peel wording (section 6); keep the peel 3–4s; lock wearing and bare frames; if it fails twice, cut lift → bare skin |
| Model invents the patch: white, beige, opaque, gel pad, tape | Early renders | Upload the real product every time; paste the PATCHES block with its "never" list |
| Under-eye pieces come out as circles or glasses-shaped outlines | Night Peel apply clip | "Just below the lower lashes, following the lid; never circles, rings or glasses shapes"; crop the placement reference to one face |
| Forehead tab points up | 5-piece sheet render | Always write "tab pointing DOWN between the eyebrows" |
| Forehead patch too small | First character shot | "Covers the middle two-thirds of the forehead; does not reach the hairline" |
| Model leaves a copy of the patch on the skin after peeling | Crow clip | End frame with the patch fully off, held in fingers beside the face, bare skin, "exactly ONE patch" |
| Patches already on before she applies them | Application shots | Separate bare-face start image; split "applying" and "wearing" into different shots |
| Patch slides, shifts or disappears | Moving shots | Less head movement, shorter shot, fix the source still |
| Clear silicone invisible | White backgrounds, flat light | Side light ~45° or backlight, tinted surface, "a thin raised edge that catches the light" |
| Hands deform | Hand-to-face shots | Simpler action, cut to a close-up, "exactly two hands with five fingers each", or real footage |
| She talks when the plan is voiceover | Week-by-week clips | "SHE DOES NOT TALK: her lips stay closed the whole time" |
| Video opens inside the reference photo's room | Gemini clips | "The cast photo is a reference for identity only; it is not the first frame; the video opens directly on shot 1" |
| A second person's face gets copied | Placement reference | "A different person; use only for placement; never copy that person's face, hair or skin" |
| Whole split-screen layout rebuilt | Multi-panel reference (105 credits wasted) | Crop every reference to one subject |
| Morning peel shot in night lamp light | Night Peel v1 | Name the light source: "soft daylight from a window on the right" |
| Skin airbrushed; wrinkles smoothed by the video model | Most video models | "Unretouched, visible pores, no beauty filter"; "her wrinkles do not change during the clip" (doesn't apply to a before/after shot) |
| Garbled reflections | Mirror scenes | No mirror in frame |
| Unwanted cream or shine on the face | Night scenes | "Skin is dry and matte, no cream or lotion" (unless the shot is about cream) |
| Bangs hide the forehead and the patch | Character portraits | "Hair pulled fully back; nothing falls on the forehead" |
| Stuttered word in native dialogue | Crow intro ("HA-HAPPEN") | No ALL CAPS in dialogue; describe emphasis; cut the glitch on frame boundaries |
| Voice changes between clips | Multi-clip presenters | Identical voice description in every prompt, same session, or one narrator re-voiced |
| Garbled on-screen text | AI-rendered text | Add all text in code or the editor |
| The CTA goes unseen | Small button under the caption area | Give the CTA its own full-screen beat |

## 10. Production pipeline and QA

Two hard gates: **no video credits until the stills pass checklist A**, and
**nothing goes live until the owner approves it.** Most wasted credits came from
skipping the first gate. A failed still is edited, never animated.

**A. Still and keyframe checklist (before any video)**
- [ ] Same person in every frame, matching the chosen character: face, hair, age, jewellery
- [ ] Product is clear and slightly frosted, never opaque; right shape and right count for the frame
- [ ] Forehead tab points down; forehead piece covers about two-thirds of the forehead and doesn't reach the hairline
- [ ] Under-eye pieces are slim crescents just below the lashes (Face Set) or the T-shape (Eye Patches), never circles or glasses
- [ ] Smile-line pieces leave the lips uncovered
- [ ] Lips closed with patches on; hair off the forehead; no mirror, cream or text in frame
- [ ] 9:16 and readable at phone size

**B. Clip checklist (after every clip)**
- [ ] Everything in checklist A still holds in every frame
- [ ] Wrinkle depth didn't change mid-clip; skin not airbrushed
- [ ] Lips never opened (unless it's a talking clip, and then the words match exactly)
- [ ] No patch slid, doubled, split, vanished or turned opaque
- [ ] Exactly two hands with five fingers; hands never pass through the face or a patch
- [ ] Starts and ends on the approved frames

**C. Final checklist (before asking for approval)**
- [ ] Price, offer and dates match the live store today
- [ ] Product visible in the first 2 seconds; the CTA has its own full-screen beat
- [ ] Text sits below the top 14% and above the bottom 35% of the frame
- [ ] Audio at −16 LUFS; music only from TikTok's Commercial Music Library, Meta's Sound Collection or another commercial-use library (business accounts can't use most trending songs)
- [ ] Link in bio and caption links point to the product page, not the homepage
- [ ] File named with concept, hook, version and date

**Log** prompts, model, settings, credits, accepted and rejected shots, and any
new lesson (append new lessons to this file).

## 11. Testing and reading results

Judge each post on whether it holds attention and then sends visitors who buy:
watch time, saves and shares, then sessions and orders in Shopify, with the
owner's visits (?utm_source=owner), relatives and bots removed. Write the
hypothesis before posting. Vary the concept, not the cosmetics. Don't call a
winner on views alone.

**Diagnose by where people drop:** poor hook or hold → change the opening;
views but few link taps → change the reason, offer or CTA; visits but no emails
or orders → expectation, trust, offer or checkout, not the creative.
