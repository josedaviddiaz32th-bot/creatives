## Marketing read (think like a marketer; cold-viewer test)
- **At 1 s** a cold viewer sees a bare-faced woman in bed holding a clear sheet up to her face, and the
  words "REAL or SUS? Wrinkle smoothing patches". She understands: *someone is testing those viral wrinkle
  patches, and I'm being asked whether it's real.* How: product + face + the question, all in frame one.
- **At 3 s** she sees the woman's forehead lines (she squints) and the bottom line "When people assume I've
  had Botox & fillers…". She understands: *these lines are the problem, and she claims patches give a
  Botox-like result.* How: the problem is on the face before the patch goes on; the caption names the
  benchmark everyone knows (Botox).
- **At 5 s** a second face pops into the corner, watching. She understands: *someone else, who looks like he
  knows, is about to judge it.* How: the stitch grammar every TikTok user knows.
- **Emotional beat:** curiosity and mild skepticism, then a small, genuine "wow" at the reveal. No drama.
- **Medium:** all live footage (UGC + a real person on camera). Ours: **all video** (owner rule).

**Q: in our version, what will an UNAWARE viewer see in the first 3–5 s that makes the problem and its cause
obvious?** This is a MOF video, so the "cause" is less important than *the problem on her face* and *the
doubt*. Openers:
- **A (pick): faithful.** 0–1 s: our P4 woman in bed, bare-faced, holds our clear forehead band up between
  the lens and her face (tab down, frosted, catching lamp light); title "REAL or SUS? Wrinkle smoothing
  patches". 1–2.5 s: she lowers it and her forehead lines show at rest. 2.5–6 s: she presses it on with two
  hands at the temples. Cold viewer by 3 s: *lines on her forehead + a clear patch going on + "real or sus?"*.
  Expert's face pops into the corner at ≈6 s.
- **B: outcome first.** Open on the morning peel and the smooth forehead, then the expert. Stronger frame
  one, but it spends the payoff before the question has done its work and breaks the reference's order.
- **C: expert first.** The expert in the corner from frame 0 squinting at the patch. Adds a face early but
  costs a longer silent expert clip (credits) and weakens the "real UGC" feel of frame one.

**Pick A**, with the corner face in at ≈4 s (v2: a 6 s silent corner clip, matching the reference's ≈3.5–4 s).

## Core mechanism
The viewer's own skepticism ("is this real?") is the hook; a live before → after is the proof; a neutral
expert-looking judge reacts, explains the mechanism in plain words and gives the verdict, so belief transfers
without the brand selling. For P4 ("mixed bag whether or not people find it worth it", "can't be real") that
is exactly the help-me-choose moment.

## Resulting plan
- **Type:** stitch / green-screen expert reaction over a two-shot UGC demo. All video.
- **Hook pattern:** on-screen skeptic question over a live product demo in frame one.
- **Pacing:** reference rhythm: UGC 0–12 s (2 shots), full-screen beat 12–15 s, green screen 15–29.5 s
  (4 short talking clips cut together = natural jump cuts every 3–5 s, plus 2 code pop-ups), end card
  29.5–30.6 s.
- **Shot style:** UGC = front-camera selfie, handheld, lamp light at night / window daylight in the morning.
  Expert = selfie-style webcam framing, generated against a flat chroma-green wall for E2–E5 (keyed in code,
  free), in an office for E1.
- **Who speaks:** only the expert (native Kling voice, same voice description in every clip). The woman never
  speaks.
- **Product timing:** patch in frame 0; name + real box at 12 s; flat lay at ≈24 s.
- **Length:** ≈30.6 s (owner).
